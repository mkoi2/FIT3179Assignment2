Environmental Sustainability
Around The World

Environmental sustainability indicators are ways to measure human impact on the Earth. It is important to uphold sustainable and environmentally friendly practices to maintain a safe and liveable planet for future generations. Key factors that will be explored within this page includes greenhouse gas emissions, renewable energy generation, waste generation and treatment and land protection and water usage.

The chart to the right illustrates the timeline of significant global goals that are currently in place by the United Nations (UN) in an effort to reduce the worst impacts of climate change.

These efforts are especially necessary as scientist have determined that global temperature needs to stay within 1.5°C of pre-industrial levels to avoid irreversible damage to the planet.

It is illustrated in the line chart that the average temperature of the earth has been steadily climbing since the pre-industrial period.

Currently the increase in temperature is already hitting the limits established by scientists due to greenhouse gas emissions. Greenhouse gases are gases that trap heat in the atmosphere and are an important factor for Earth to sustain life, however with human's industrial activities the amount of gases being released into the atmosphere is too great leading to global warming and climate change.

Therefore, it is crucial that nations come together and prioritise environmental sustainability. This webpage will highlight the country's leading the way to a sustainable future and the countries that still have ways to go.

Greenhouse Gas Emissions

The main gases that make up greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide. The contribution of each of these gases to global greenhouse gas emissions can be observed in the waffle chart.

carbon dioxide is the most prominent gas emitted. It is produced when fossil fuels are burned, industrial production and land use changes.

methane is produced within agriculture, fossil fuel production and waste decomposition in landfills.

nitrous oxide is primarily produced from agriculture, when nitrogen fertilizers treat the soils.

Waste Generation and Treatment

The OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) is an international organisation that works to build better policies for sustainable economic growth, trade and higher living standards worldwide. This organisation was founded in 1961 and has 38 countries as its members.

Below explores the waste generation and treatment undertaken by the member countries of the OECD.

Sending waste to landfill is extremely damaging for the environment as it releases greenhouse gases and requires lots of land and drives away wildlife, therefore more efforts need to be made to reduce this method of treatment. Incinerating waste to treat it does release greenhouse gases but this treatment method does convert non-recyclable waste into energy which offsets the need to burn as much fossil fuels. Therefore, if energy recovery is accompanied with this method, it is a solution to reduce landfill waste. To make true sustainable initiatives more countries should follow suit with South Korea, Slovenia, Netherlands and Germany which have high rates of recycling and composting of their waste.

Protected Areas

Protected areas are spaces which are managed to conserve the natural ecosystem or cultural heritage.

Countries with the most protected land by area are displayed in the chart to the left. Australia ranked 3rd in the world in 2024 with about 14,575 terrestrial protected areas and 319 marine protected areas.
